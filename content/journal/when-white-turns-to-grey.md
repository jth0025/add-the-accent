My white has turned to Grey…my color is better seen against the calming yet crashing Pacific of the grey. Focus is intentional and carved specifically to you and who you are designed to be.

Canvas is essential to your work. The feel, the thickness, the origin. Why grey? Grey is powerful because it is relatively neutral. Surround a saturated red, electric blue, acid green, amber, or violet with grey and the eye becomes more aware of that color's intensity. Grey isn't fighting for attention, so it becomes a kind of visual silence around the note you actually want people to hear. Grey is Marvin to the Troubled Man. The shadows arrive before the Man. Grey is the shadow. Grey feels like something has already happened.

It carries shadow. History. Atmosphere. It exists somewhere between light and darkness, warm and cool, finished and unfinished. It doesn't demand an answer from me. It gives me somewhere to begin.

And when color finally enters, grey knows how to get out of its way.

Red becomes more alive. Green becomes richer. Yellow finds warmth. Blue becomes deeper. Against grey, color doesn't merely decorate the space. It announces itself.

Maybe that's why I'm drawn to it.

Grey reminds me that creation doesn't always have to begin with a blank canvas.

Sometimes you begin in the middle.

From white to Grey; chaos flees. We've begun the work. When white turns to grey.
