It’s such a peculiar thing; these digital demons. How they thrive and move with such stealth and vigor. How they’ve evolved into the modern-day bully.

A few characters sent to your cell phone. A comment under a thread. A DM in your inbox. They arrive without a face, glowing in the dark of your palm; small tools, really, for sending a devious ploy of attack with little cost and even less reward.

It makes me look back at my older encounters, when I was a young pup; a scrawny bean. Back then, the enemy had a face and a fist, and when it was over, it was over.

A quick scuffle in the yard. A few swings in the bathroom. A backyard beatdown, and you’re on the Nintendo 64 by 6. I agreed to fight; most times, that is. And everything ended after said fight. No lingering. No wondering. I could handle situations like that so much better.

I had to fight every day for a while, candidly speaking.

Literally, every day.

I feel a slight hesitation calling my bully an actual… bully, because even he knew I would fight him. Maybe with restraint, but I was always going to give him a good squabble, nonetheless. He just believed he would win every time, and I guess he was soundly accurate. But unfortunately for him, he would ALWAYS have to prove his worth. I never considered the journey of my bully. What was tormenting him? How did he come to this crossroad? How did God place me in front of him and him before me? Back then there was no room for any of that. There was only the next time he would run into me.

NOT fighting simply wasn’t an option; unless I was already in the safety of my home and you were looking for me to come outside. Now, why would I do that? “You’re just gonna have to catch me when you see me” was always my sentiment. I never welcomed the confrontation; just stayed ready for it.

It got so bad that we had to have a family meeting.

What am I gonna do?

I’m tired of fighting.

Exhausted.

Everyone wants to fight me it seemed, and I don’t provoke anyone.

Initially.

That was an unfortunate version of myself. A true victim, and yet the provocateur of giants. Two kids grappling in the same yard; mine was a puddle, theirs an ocean of trauma. I couldn’t fathom what monsters lurked in the depths of a bully’s ocean.

But wait, let me back up. I never had to do this until my dad and I were exiled from our former lives. At least, that’s how I always saw it. My family was peaceful in my younger years. Until my dad remarried. But even those waves subsided at some point. Peace found us again after a devastating hurricane season.

In that meeting, my new mom introduced some tricky words to me. Almost like spells. She was EXCELLENT with wordplay. Verbal chaos, if you will. She would give me some examples, and as a kid, I was pretty impressed. That was pretty clever, I thought. I could try this out. Fighting with words was now the conclusion.

And so it began; my journey to becoming a Verbal Samurai. Killing these ninjas with logic and wit, reason and spin, vocab and punchline.

In battle, I garnered the respect of my peers and spectators, but never the bully. Never the Final Boss. It only enraged them more. I still had to fight the traditional way for a while, but it dissipated once I became consumed with my own passions that would later take me away to my own hidden fantasyland.

But a blade doesn’t care who holds it. The same words I trained with would one day find their way back to me, only now I could no longer see who was swinging.

Fast forward to today.

The swordsmen are now shadow fighters. The attacks are now drenched in deception. Clothed in spin and devious dissent. A lie here and a truth bend there. Words sent at 100 miles an hour, with no clear sender on the horizon.

Alas, I surrender. I’m not interested in fighting anymore. Peace is indeed my jam now. But I’m not sure if Peace is feeling me, or if violence is obsessed with me.

One could deduce that Peace is a treasure requiring a long pilgrimage to be obtained. Violence is a short road paved with sugar, and every tongue knows the way.

It’s quick. It’s sweet. It hits the tongue and the bloodstream at the exact same time, and for a moment you feel powerful, justified, finished. The punch lands. The comment posts. The last word gets sent.

Relief.

Then the crash comes, and nobody ever remembers the ingredients.

Peace offers no such rush. Peace asks you to stay in the room with the discomfort, long enough to repair it. So people reach for the sweeter thing. And leaving is sweet, too. The block button. The unanswered message. The door that clicks shut behind you. It feels like peace for about a minute; it tastes like freedom. But it is only another way to swing; a punch thrown from a distance, and the one on the receiving end is left holding the blow with no one left to ask what they did.

Silence is violent.

Can you imagine a friendship ending over a single text? Someone accuses you of something you can’t make sense of, then tells you, “You know exactly what you did.” And you’re sitting there rereading the message because you really, truly don’t. It’s a complete mind trip. They took the sweet exit, and I was left with the bitter after.

Are there moments when retreat is the best course of action? Absolutely. Maybe you’ve tried to talk before and every conversation left you feeling smaller. Maybe staying long enough to explain yourself gives someone another chance to hurt you. I can understand why a person would choose the door. And when a conversation can happen safely, I think a friendship deserves at least one honest sentence about what went wrong. Knowing how to leave and knowing when to leave are two different kinds of wisdom. Sugar melts quick, and a person can spend a long time trying to name what’s left on their tongue.

And now, here we are. Technology advances our everyday lives and our trauma. We share it like the tastiest new snack, without reading the ingredients; and the first ingredient is always sugar. We share evil before we share love. We go silent before we try to connect and repair. All of these dirty paths and roads.

And somewhere on the deep horizon of all this melee, an annoying streak of empathy blazes.

I hate empathy now, because I can’t escape it. I set out to place myself in the shoes of my offenders, even the ones who fled, and saw myself in all of them to some degree. There is a freedom in that; and an imprisonment as well. I feel alone in this experience.

I understand the door, however: that glowing outline of escape. But every one of them chose it. Not one stayed long enough to say what I did. That’s how I know which road was easier.

All this plainly means there’s more work for me. More adjusting and more conceding. I must suffer to agree. It’s the only blow I take now. To agree is to meet someone where they stand, even when they have never taken a single step towards you. To acquiesce; not the silent kind that gives in and disappears, but the kind that stays in the room. I once agreed to fight. Now agreement asks for something harder. All of it makes no sense in my carnal mind, but it aligns with some divine purpose. How annoying. How unfair.

That kind of work is heavy when you have nothing to hold onto. I don’t remember the last time I had such an anchor. My dad was my anchor. My family was my anchor. That was a long time ago. I find myself looking to the sky far more often. Oddly enough, I can hear some semblance of His voice more clearly up there. His image remains painted vividly in the nimbus. Somehow, things may always work themselves out. But I always remember the fatigue. The weariness.

I’m tired.

I’m tired of fighting everyone. Anyone.

I really want to love again.

Give. Receive. Exchange. Rest. Repeat.

Make music. Make love. Smile. Sing. Paint. Tell stories and enjoy the wonders of God with another.

You’d have to be crazy not to see that in me. I’m convinced of that; it’s probably the only thing that keeps me sane.

Old concepts in a new digital age.

Maybe I should create a Love App. What would that even look like?

All I know is that there would be a lot of agreement in it.
